## Apply the relationships to live-action footage

Build a small, readable action unit: venue/activity context → preparation/detail → release/contact → result/reaction → next activity. Preserve event/person continuity where it is available. A reaction from an unrelated attempt should be logged as a montage association rather than assumed proof of that specific result.

Let some musical attacks land on in-shot action or a short graphic accent. Use a new cut when viewpoint, scale or activity needs to change. For an intense brief, combine short bursts with a few readable holds; the observed reference still gives the viewer spatial anchors.

For JUMP's provisional 29–73 s excerpt, the 29–33 s low-energy lead can prepare one activity; 33–46 s can contain two or three complete action units rather than an unrelated new shot on every candidate peak. The 56–60 s drop can show a readable response or prepare the next unit. This is a treatment hypothesis; it does not change the unverified musical boundaries or approve the final excerpt length.

## Adobe implementation and review recipe

1. **Premiere:** keep original camera clips, assign markers to inspected preparation/event/recovery, and plan scale changes between shots. Keep music and protected regions as the current brief requires. Select the actual action before adding transitions.
2. **AE, when graphics are allowed:** use a separate original Shape path for a directional accent. Place the contact origin on the inspected object location. Use a short Trim Paths reveal and opacity decay; those native primitives have been rendered in this laboratory. Moving attachment needs tracked or manually keyed positions and fresh verification; that is not already solved by the static pose test.
3. **Foreground occlusion:** an actual moving arm, ball or game element may motivate a brief mask/wipe. Preserve the observed movement direction and incoming attention position. Treat a proposed camera/particle recreation as an untested recipe until it has been built on the real footage. Avoid adding combat props/emotional stakes to a cheerful venue simply to copy the source.
4. **Illustration/source design:** retain editable Shape/Path layers or original vector/raster artwork. Choose AE for the already-tested 2D accents and compositing. Elaborate scene lighting/3D interactions require a separate scope and native proof; the reference movie alone does not establish the original software or a guaranteed one-effect recreation.
5. **QA:** inspect before the event, the event, several frames after, and the first settled frame after the next cut. Compare silhouette, attachment, foreground occlusion, direction and recovery. Check generated graphics at their own render rate, then at the delivery rate after retiming. Inspect reference flash timing without importing its flashing pattern as a default.

Prompt addition: “For each scene, state its story function and evidence. Mark shot changes, in-shot accents, camera movement and uncertain occluded changes separately. Build preparation/action/reaction units from the supplied footage. Keep music candidates and ASR leads unverified until checked; preserve source PTS, sampling stride and frame-rate uncertainty. Explain the effect's attachment and the exact frame where the incoming shot becomes readable.”
